UK GDPR and AI Regulation in Financial Services

LLM Regulation & Compliance · Queen Mary University of London · 2025

Financial services firms are adopting AI faster than the rules around it are settling. Most UK firms already use it in some form. But every model that scores a customer, flags a transaction or answers a query runs on personal data, and the UK GDPR still applies to every one of those uses.

My dissertation looks at where those two worlds meet: what data protection law already demands of AI in financial services, where the gaps are, and what firms should do before a regulator or a customer asks.

  • i

    AI risk is already regulated

    The FCA hasn't written new AI rules. It relies on Consumer Duty, SM&CR and data protection law, so firms need to understand the rules they already have.

  • ii

    Automated decisions need a human story

    Credit, fraud and onboarding decisions made by models have to be explainable and open to challenge by the people they affect.

  • iii

    Governance is a competitive edge

    Firms that can show how their AI uses data win the trust of customers, regulators and clients, and can grow faster because of it.

Using AI safely in a regulated firm

I designed and delivered AI training for colleagues at my firm. The aim was simple: help people get real value from AI tools without putting client data, confidentiality or the firm's regulatory position at risk.

The training influenced firm policy and sharpened our risk appetite, and I've since shared it with other firms. Tools don't create risk on their own; people's habits do. That's why training matters as much as the technology.
